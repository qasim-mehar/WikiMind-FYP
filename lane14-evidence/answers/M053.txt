question: Which papers use BLEU and which use Exact Match or human evaluation?
answer:   The BLEU metric is used in the paper 'Attention is all you need', where the Transformer model achieves higher BLEU scores than previous state-of-the-art models [c-1706-03762-attention, c-e02ff7434fc3]. Specifically, the Transformer model achieved a BLEU score of 41.8 on the WMT 2014 English-to-French translation task, while the Transformer (big) model achieved 41.0 on the same task and 28.4 on the WMT 2014 English-to-German task [c-82789749ad65, c-276a1ea04b43, c-e2c686bf6eac]. Additionally, label smoothing is noted to improve BLEU scores [c-4a5f1287b1ee], and single-head attention performance is measured in BLEU points [c-eb90f687d8a1]. The RAG paper reports using the Q-BLEU-1 metric for Jeopardy question generation [c-9c758c0f3b8b]. Exact Match (EM) scores are reported as an evaluation metric in the study [c-ebfce1167c41]. Human evaluation is also utilized; for instance, human evaluators prefer text generated by RAG models over BART models [c-3432b4a6f52d], and evaluators find RAG generations to be more specific [c-8157b0324bee]. Figure 4 in the RAG paper displays an annotation interface used for the human evaluation of factuality [c-4eb326a7b6bc].
